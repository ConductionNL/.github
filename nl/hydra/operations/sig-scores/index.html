Quality matrix

This page is our own measurement, not Sigrid. We measure each app's development branch with our gates and tools. The SIG rows estimate what the Software Improvement Group (SIG) will say in Sigrid. Sigrid uses its own parsers and scoping, so its numbers can differ.

Measured on 2026-11-06. The commit under each app name is the head that was measured. An app we measured again later shows its own date under its name. For that app, the rating, SIG maintainability rows and levers come from that date. Its other rows keep the date above.

Why the scores jump from 1 November 2026

Until 31 October 2026 the fleet followed the Nextcloud coding standards. From 1 November 2026 we apply the SIG maintainability rules of the SIG/TÜV NORD guidance. Gates 122 and 123 check them on every change. That is why the scores rise from 1 November.

How to read the matrix​

Each column is an app. The rows come in five sections.

  1. Estimated SIG rating. Our estimate of the rating Sigrid would give, from 0.5 to 5.5. The overall row is a range. Below it are the five sub-characteristics and the seven properties. All of it is an estimate.
  2. SIG maintainability. Every check of gate 122, per language, rated from 1 to 5 stars.
  3. SIG's other models. Open Source Health and Architecture Quality, where we can measure them. SIG publishes only the 4-star value, so a cell says met (4★) or missed (<4).
  4. Our own measures. Tests, debt, security, and accessibility and UI. We define these bands ourselves. The table below the matrix gives each band's basis.
  5. Gates per category. Each row shows the gates that pass out of the gates that apply, and the findings of all of them. Open a row to see its gates.
  • Unit size, complexity and interfacing: the share of unit code above the bound, for example the code in units longer than 15 lines.
  • Duplication: the share of redundant lines, in fragments of 6 lines or more. In a Vue file we treat template, script and style as separate pieces, so a fragment never crosses from one into the next. This is our assumption until a real Sigrid scan confirms it.
  • Module coupling: the share of module code in files with more than 10, 20 or 50 incoming dependencies.
  • Component independence: the share of code that no other component calls. Higher is better here. For every other check, lower is better.

A cell shows the stars and the measured value. Hover a cell for the details. An empty cell (·) means the app has no such code or the gate does not apply.

SIG maintainability bands​

The 4-star limits come from the SIG/TÜV NORD CERT Guidance for Producers v18.1, dated 31 March 2026. It is the 2026 model. Volume no longer counts.

SIG publishes no other level. We derive the 5, 3 and 2-star limits ourselves. The only public table of all levels is Alves, Correia and Visser (2011). We take its distance between each band and the 4-star band. We apply that ratio to the v18.1 limits. Duplication uses the band ratios of Heitlager, Kuipers and Visser (2007). Anything worse than 2 stars is 1 star.

How we estimate the SIG rating​

SIG rates each app from 0.5 to 5.5 stars. Our estimate follows SIG's own steps, from the checks up to one number. Where SIG publishes nothing, we made a choice. Each choice is listed under assumptions.

  1. Check. A share of code between two star limits gets a rating between them, on a straight line. A share at the 4-star limit rates 3.5. This is equation 1 of Alves, Correia and Visser (2011). We test it on the paper's own example, which rates 2.68.
  2. Property. A property with three checks takes its worst check. Same paper.
  3. Languages. Each language gets its own property rating. We weight them by the code each property measures in that language.
  4. Sub-characteristic. The plain mean of its properties. Analysability: duplication and unit size. Modifiability: duplication, unit complexity and module coupling. Testability: unit complexity and component independence. Modularity: module coupling, component independence and component entanglement. Reusability: unit size and unit interfacing.
  5. Overall. The power mean of the five sub-characteristics. SIG switched to a power mean in 2026 but does not publish the exponent. We show two: p = 1, the plain mean, and p = -1, the harmonic mean. The harmonic mean pulls harder towards the weakest sub-characteristic. The real rating probably sits between them.

Rounding to stars follows SIG: half up, and 5.5 is still 5 stars. A rating row is coloured by its stars and shows the value. The overall row is coloured by the lower end of its range.

Sources​

  • SIG, Key changes in the 2026 SIG quality models: volume is removed, and the overall rating is now a power mean.
  • SIG/TÜV NORD CERT, Guidance for Producers v18.1, 31 March 2026: the 4-star limits, per language.
  • SIG/TÜViT, Evaluation Criteria Trusted Product Maintainability v12.0, 2020: which properties feed which sub-characteristic, and the order of the steps.
  • Alves, Correia and Visser, Benchmark-based aggregation of metrics to ratings, IWSM/Mensura 2011: the interpolation and the minimum over checks.
  • Baggen, Correia, Schill and Visser, Standardized code quality benchmarking for improving software maintainability, Software Quality Journal 2012: a sub-characteristic is the mean of its properties, and the overall rating the mean of the sub-characteristics.
  • SIG's own example in the 2026 changes page fits this: its overall 3.2 is the mean of the sub-characteristics, not of the properties.

Assumptions​

  • Exponent. SIG does not publish it. We show p = 1 and p = -1 as a range.
  • Weights. Every property counts equally in its sub-characteristic, and every sub-characteristic in the overall rating. SIG's criteria say a panel of experts sets the weights, and SIG does not publish them. SIG's example fits equal weights.
  • Mapping in 2026. The 2020 mapping, minus volume and component balance, which the 2026 model no longer rates. Nothing is added.
  • Power mean at the last step only. The sub-characteristics stay a plain mean. SIG's text does not say at which step the power mean applies.
  • Languages. Weighted by code. SIG says most results are weighted by code volume, but not how languages combine.
  • 1 star. Below the 2-star limit the rating falls on a straight line to 0.5 when all code is in the band. The paper does not give this end.
  • Limits other than 4 stars. Our derived 5, 3 and 2-star limits from the table above.
  • Independence and entanglement below 4 stars. SIG rates them below 4 stars but publishes only the 4-star limit. For the estimate, each star level doubles the allowed exposure. Independence: at least 97.25, 94.5, 89 and 78% hidden code for 5, 4, 3 and 2 stars. Entanglement: at most 0.0375, 0.075, 0.15 and 0.30. The check rows keep showing <4.
  • Entanglement value. We rate the violation degree. SIG combines it with the communication density against benchmark maxima it does not publish.
  • Missing structure. Module coupling, independence and entanglement are measured for PHP under lib/ and for Python only. JavaScript, TypeScript and Vue count for the unit checks and duplication.

Levers per app​

A lever is one property in one language. For each lever we move the next 1,000 lines above a bound to low risk, worst band first, and rate the app again. The gain is how far the overall rating rises, under both exponents. When fewer than 1,000 lines are left, the gain is for the lines that are left.

Lines for +0.5 is the fewest lines to fix in that language to raise the property by half a star. "Not by vue alone" means fixing every vue line is not enough, because the other languages hold the rating down.

A lever is sure when it ranks in the top 3 under both exponents. The panel shows the top 5 of each app, ordered by their two ranks together. Start with a sure lever.

What we add beyond SIG​

SIG rates maintainability, security, open source health and architecture. It does not rate tests, debt hidden in baselines, or accessibility. These are our own measures, with the reason we add each one and the basis of its bands.

Spec coverage counts the methods gate 16 holds in scope, with and without an @spec tag. E2e coverage is gate 19's report over the scenarios that are not excluded. PHP coverage is the statement coverage in the newest clover report CI kept for the app: from development if there is one, otherwise from the newest run on another branch. The tooltip names the branch, commit and date. CI keeps these reports 14 days. Without a readable report the cell falls back to the coverage-guard floor in .coverage-baseline, says stale with the date that floor last changed, and the run prints a warning. The vulnerable dependency count treats a Composer advisory without a severity as high, because nothing shows it is lower. The gate rows come from a full-scope run of every gate on development.

The matrix reads website/src/data/sig-matrix.json. One script writes it.

  1. Clone each app's development branch into one directory, named after the app.

  2. Run gate 122 in report mode on each clone:

    python3 hydra-gates/scripts/lib/check_sig_maintainability.py report <clones>/<app> \
      --json <report-dir>/<app>.json

  3. Run all gates at full scope on each clone. The runner writes gates.json into its log directory:

    HYDRA_GATE_LOG_DIR=<gates-dir>/<app> bash hydra-gates/scripts/run-hydra-gates.sh --full <clones>/<app>

  4. Run the generator from the repository root. It runs composer audit and npm audit on the lockfiles and reads the CI coverage reports with gh api, so it needs the network and a logged-in gh:

    python3 website/scripts/build-sig-matrix.py <report-dir> --clones <clones> --gates <gates-dir> --date YYYY-MM-DD

  5. Commit the new sig-matrix.json.

The generator takes the rating estimate and the star bands from gate 122 and the own bands from website/scripts/quality_measures.py. A change to either shows up in the matrix on the next refresh.
